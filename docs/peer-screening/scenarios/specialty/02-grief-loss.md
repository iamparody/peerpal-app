# Scenario: The First Year
**Skill:** Grief & Loss Support (`grief_and_loss_support`)  
**Version:** 1 | **Status:** DRAFT — pending clinical review  
**Estimated time:** 9–11 minutes

---

## Setup

Someone who lost a parent eight months ago is reaching out because people in their life keep telling them they should be "over it" by now. This scenario tests whether you understand that grief has no timeline, that presence is more valuable than advice, that comparing losses is harmful, and that the goal is not to accelerate their healing but to accompany them in it.

Prerequisite: All 5 baseline skills

---

## Scoring Rubric

| Tag | Points | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| `no_timeline` | +15 | Affirming that grief has no schedule |
| `presence` | +10 | Being with them in the difficulty without fixing |
| `validation` | +10 | Affirming their feeling as understandable |
| `open_question` | +10 | Inviting them to share more in their own words |
| `grief_timeline` | −20 | Implying they should be further along by now |
| `silver_lining` | −15 | Introducing a positive reframe they didn't ask for |
| `comparison` | −15 | Comparing their loss to someone else's experience |
| `advice_giving` | −10 | Telling them what to do with their grief |

**Pass threshold:** 65 points out of 100

---

## Node 1 — Opening

**They say:**  
*"My dad died eight months ago. I still cry every day. My friends keep saying I need to move on but I don't know how."*

**Choose your response:**

**A)** "Eight months isn't very long at all — and there's no point at which you're supposed to have stopped grieving."  
→ `no_timeline` +15 → **Node 2A**

**B)** "Grief takes time. Have you tried journaling about him?"  
→ `advice_giving` −10 → **Node 2B**

**C)** "I'm so sorry. What was your dad like?"  
→ `open_question` +10, `presence` +10 → **Node 2A**

**D)** "Eight months — yes, most people start to feel better around that point. Maybe you need more support."  
→ `grief_timeline` −20 → **Node 2C**

---

## Node 2A — Good start

**They say:**  
*"He was everything. He was the person I called when anything happened — good or bad. And now there's just this silence where he used to be."*

**Choose your response:**

**A)** "That silence — that's one of the most painful parts of grief. The habit of reaching for someone who's no longer there."  
→ `validation` +10, `presence` +10 → **Node 3A**

**B)** "You'll find other people to call over time."  
→ `silver_lining` −15 → **Node 3B**

**C)** "That's a beautiful way to describe it. What was something you'd normally call him about?"  
→ `open_question` +10 → **Node 3A**

**D)** "My friend lost her mum last year and she found that starting a new routine helped."  
→ `comparison` −15, `advice_giving` −10 → **Node 3B**

---

## Node 2B — After advice (recovery)

**They say:**  
*"I've tried journaling. It doesn't help."*

**Recovery:**

**A)** "That makes sense — there's no single thing that works, and nothing is supposed to fix this anyway. What does help, even a little?"  
→ `open_question` +10, `no_timeline` +10 → **Node 3A**

**B)** "Maybe you need to give it more time."  
→ `advice_giving` −10 → **Node 3C**

---

## Node 2C — After timeline comment (recovery)

**They say:**  
*"So you think I should be over it too."*

**Recovery:**

**A)** "No — I got that wrong. There's no 'supposed to be' with grief. Eight months, eight years — grief doesn't follow a schedule and I shouldn't have implied it did."  
→ `no_timeline` +15 (recovery) → **Node 3A**

**B)** "I was just saying that professionally speaking, extended grief can be a sign of something more."  
→ `clinical_claim` −15 → **Node 3C (fail)**

---

## Node 3A — Strong path

**They say:**  
*"Sometimes I feel guilty for having good days. Like I'm forgetting him if I laugh."*

**Choose your response:**

**A)** "Guilt for laughing — that's one of the strangest and most common parts of grief. Laughing doesn't mean you've forgotten him."  
→ `validation` +10 → **Node 4 (end)**

**B)** "He'd want you to be happy."  
→ `silver_lining` −15 → **Node 4 (partial)**

**C)** "What do you think he'd say to you about that?"  
→ `open_question` +10 → **Node 4 (end)**

**D)** "You shouldn't feel guilty. Happiness is healthy."  
→ `advice_giving` −10 (dismissing the feeling) → **Node 4 (partial)**

---

## Node 3B — Silver lining / comparison path

**They say:**  
*"That doesn't really help."*

**Recovery:**

**A)** "You're right — I'm sorry. I don't want to compare or rush you past what you're feeling. What do you need right now?"  
→ `presence` +10, `open_question` +10 → **Node 4 (partial)**

**B)** "I was just trying to help you see the positive."  
→ no recovery → **Node 4 (fail)**

---

## Node 3C — Fail path

→ **Node 4 (end, fail)**

---

## Node 4 — End

**Pass (≥65):** *"You stayed with them in the grief rather than trying to move them out of it. That's the whole skill — grief doesn't need to be fixed, only accompanied."*

**Near pass (45–64):** *"You were mostly present. [Lowest tag]. The impulse to comfort someone by offering a silver lining or a comparison is natural, but it often lands as a message that their grief is inconvenient. Staying in the feeling with them is more useful."*

**Fail (<45):** *"This scenario tests whether you can resist the pull to fix, reframe, or rush grief. Telling someone they should be further along, or that others have had it worse, closes the conversation. The goal is presence — not resolution."*
